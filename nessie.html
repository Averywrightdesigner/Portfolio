Introduction

Students deserve a safe online space for regulating their mental health that is both secure and private. The tools that already existed were either too complex or locked behind paywalls.

  • Project Manager
  • User interviewer
  • Prototyper
  • Illustrator

My role

Project goals

  1. 01

    Create a private, secure space where students can check in on their mental health.

  2. 02

    Keep it simple and free to use, unlike the tools already out there.

Process

Research

7 interviews, 7 products

Our team ran a literature review, studied 7 similar apps, and interviewed 7 students and recent grads. Most cared about saving time and keeping their information private.

My interview

Habits, design, and privacy

I interviewed a college student about how she copes with stress and what would make her trust an app. Her answers shaped four of my screens.

Persona

Meet Faye

We combined the interviews into Faye, a 22-year-old pre-med student who works part-time and forgets to check her to-do list.

What she told meWhat I designed

  • Deep breaths got her through her last overwhelming moment at work.

    →

    A guided breathing exercise, plus a screen explaining why it works

  • Habits like journaling fall off when she's tired.

    →

    Streaks and rewards that take seconds a day

  • She wants an app to feel safe and secure.

    →

    Login with Face ID or a 4-digit PIN

  • Seeing a profile for the person she's talking to builds trust.

    →

    Nine Nessie profile icons, so no one has to share a photo

Illustrations

I drew every Nessie

She has a different expression for each part of the app. She looks stressed in onboarding, peeks in to say hello, and jumps out of the water when you keep a streak. I also drew the nine profile icons and a sticker.

Testing

We tested with 3 participants ages 18 to 22. Each one thought out loud through 6 tasks, then filled out a System Usability Scale survey.

Challenges

  • Every participant tried to use features we hadn't prototyped yet, like typing a journal entry.
  • Streaks and points got more excitement than we expected, and participants wanted more from them.

Solutions

  • We added working interactions to the sign in, journal, community, and profile pages.
  • Streaks and achievements were redesigned to feel more like a game. I prototyped those screens and animations.

Result

6 / 6

tasks completed by participants with little to no help
